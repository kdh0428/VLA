Proof of concept · two driving VLAs

Encoded but Unused

Both ORION and AutoVLA hold a confident action decision when they drive wrongly — the decision just isn’t the one their own perceptual evidence implies. The failure is misalignment, not missing information.

ORION · Bench2Drive · 1,809 frames / 49 clips AutoVLA · nuPlan navtest · 2,748 scenes / 28 logs 0 inference failures
01

What the two studies actually established

The question was whether a driving VLA can encode the scene correctly and still emit the wrong trajectory, and where inside the network that link breaks. ORION answered the descriptive half; the causal half failed; AutoVLA was run to test whether any of it is architecture-specific.

Holds

Wrong actions are common while perception is intact. In ORION, P+A− is 23.2% of all frames and 55.6% of every action failure, stable at 19–35% across the full threshold sweep and present in 39 of 42 scenarios.

Holds

Failing representations are not empty. In both models the failing hidden state decodes the model’s own action far better than the correct one. This is the cross-architecture result.

Fails

No causal handle was found. Editing ORION’s planning token — donor patch or mean-difference steering — recovers no more trajectories than a random edit of the same magnitude (7.5% vs 5.5%, Fisher p = 0.54).

Blocked

The ORION taxonomy does not port to AutoVLA. The released RFT checkpoint emitted chain-of-thought in 1 of 2,748 scenes, so there is no stated perception and no observable reasoning to label.

02

A correction that changed the conclusion

Corrected 2026-09-09

The first ORION report concluded that failing samples “do not contain the action decision.” That inference was circular. A± is defined by agreement between the model’s action and the ground-truth action, so decoding the GT action necessarily succeeds in A+ and fails in A−.

Re-running the probe for the model’s own predicted action settles it.

Two things follow. The control columns are identical by construction — they carry no independent information and should never have been quoted as evidence. And in the failure group the model’s own action is decodable at 0.94–0.96 from L16 onward while the correct action sits at 0.45–0.79.

So perceptual evidence is encoded (0.83–0.91) and an action is encoded (0.94–0.96) — they simply disagree. That also explains the null intervention: a model already committed to a different action by mid-stack will not flip when nudged along a group-mean axis.

AutoVLA’s dominant failure is a different one

Because AutoVLA emits its trajectory as ordinary vocabulary tokens, the logit lens applies directly — an analysis ORION structurally cannot support. It shows the action decision forming only in the last ~4 of 36 layers, then propagating: once one action token is wrong the next is wrong 73% of the time, against 4% when the prefix is clean.

04

What would overturn this

Primary confound

Architecture and benchmark are not separated. ORION ran on adversarial CARLA scenarios, AutoVLA on smooth real-world nuPlan where a constant-acceleration baseline already reaches 0.90 m. The 5× gap in failure rate is at least partly task difficulty. Nothing here attributes the difference to architecture alone.

Two further limits are load-bearing. AutoVLA’s perception probes rest on 28 logs, with lead-vehicle positives in only 17 of them — below the cross-scene bar the ORION study adopted, so those numbers are flagged rather than headline. And in ORION the late-layer geometric scores correlate with ADE at ρ ≈ −0.45, so they partly restate the error they are meant to explain.

The A− label in AutoVLA is also softer than it looks: with ADE median 0.038 m, most failures are adjacent-category confusions such as MAINTAIN → DECELERATE, not dangerous driving.

05

Recommended next steps

Ordered by how much each one changes what can be claimed, not by effort. The first two are prerequisites for publishing any cross-architecture statement at all.

  1. Separate architecture from benchmark

    Run AutoVLA on its CARLA or Waymo benchmark, or on a difficulty-matched navtest slice. Until one model is evaluated on both regimes, the headline comparison in §3 has an uncontrolled variable and no causal reading is defensible.

    largest threat to every current claim · ~1 day

  2. Teacher-forced action-token counterfactual

    Replace a wrong action token at step t with the ground-truth token and regenerate the remainder. The propagation effect is so large (4% → 73%) that this converts the strongest AutoVLA finding from correlational to causal at very low cost — and it is the one intervention the null ORION result does not already rule out.

    highest value per hour · ~2 GPU-hours

  3. Re-run the ORION intervention with a supervised direction

    Difference-of-means was the weakest available estimator and it failed. The trained action-probe weight vector reaches 0.99 AUROC on controls and is the natural replacement. Widen the surface beyond <waypoint_ego> to the visual and reasoning spans, and raise n to 150–200 failures — 25 resolves only about ±5 pp.

    ~6–8 GPU-hours

  4. Attention analysis where the decision is actually formed

    AutoVLA settles its answer in the final ~4 layers. Trace how perception tokens feed those layers — attention from action positions to critical-object positions, and head ablation. This is where “encoded but unused” becomes a mechanism rather than a description.

    follows from step 2 · ~1 day
06

The claim worth defending

Across two architectures, a driving VLA that acts wrongly is not one that has lost the information. It holds the scene and a confident decision, and the two do not agree.

That is a narrower claim than the one the first ORION report made, and it survives the circularity check that broke the original. It is also the claim the next round of experiments is designed to make causal rather than descriptive.
